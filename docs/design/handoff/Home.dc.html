The open financial-data commons

The people's financial data, returned to the people.

US financial-disclosure data — congressional trades, institutional holdings — pulled only from primary government sources, republished with its receipts. Others sell this free layer back at $30–200/month; here the public record itself is free forever. Convenience layers (alerts, integrations) may someday be paid — the data never will be.

The differentiator isn't coverage — anyone can scrape these filings. It's that every number here tells you exactly how stale it is, what it excludes, and how it was derived.

The data is free forever
MIT-licensed code, per-source data conditions recorded and honored. Every row links to the government document it came from; every build is verifiable by hash.
Honest about its own gaps
Parse coverage, unjoined names, paper filings awaiting OCR — published on the methodology page, which is a product surface here, not a footer link.
No tracking, no account required
No cookies, no profiles, nothing gated behind sign-up. Watchlists stay in your browser. Also available as an MIT-licensed MCP server for analysts working inside an LLM client.
